The joy of awareness
<2026-10-31T09:01:42:00+03:00> :metaphysics:consciousness:essay:philosophy:

Table of Contents

  • 1. Defining terms
  • 2. Bounds of consciousness
  • 3. Analysing the infinite

One might already know the idiom 'train of thought' or 'stream of thought,' which is used to describe how consciousness progresses over time, as a continuous progression of thoughts reaching a certain conclusion. I believe it's very descriptive about the nature of thoughts, and it's very convenient to describe conscious experience. It however does birth the question: How else can we describe and analyse conscious experience, and what else can we know about the processes our mind conducts so that we can experience these vast ranges of thoughts, emotions, and sensory experience?

This is an introduction to how I view conscious experience, and how I tried to diminish the 'black-box' aspect of mind.

1. Defining terms

Firstly, one should be familiar with what consciousness is, and what it extends to. Consciousness can be simply defined as the individual's capacity of joint awareness of internal and external environments. Conscious experience can be defined as experience that combines both internal and external events and states.

From this definition, one could already understand that consciousness is analytical by nature, aware and always increasing in awareness through the process of analysis. It should follow, that conscious experience is the enactment of this continuous process of consciousness analysing external environments, and introspecting into internal environments similarly.

Conscious experience is, as the idiom shows, a body ever extending, ever extended. And at any one moment, we are experiencing stages of an ever-unfolding stream of events. The name for the unit that composes the body of consciousness is the thought.

2. Bounds of consciousness

This is as far as the traditional understanding of consciousness can go: that we are experiencing thought after thought, each related to its adjacent previous and forthcoming. And that these thoughts themselves are not whole, but parts of a process which bears an distant potential conclusion.

Firstly, we understand that the process is bounded by time, by stages as represented by thought, and by transitions between these stages. One cannot exactly experience a definite boundary between thoughts, as the chronology of consciousness is continuous and not definite. Instead, every thought blends into the other, and does not exist as definite, but is analysed as a definite body.

Secondly, we understand from what is told that consciousness is composite, implying its finiteness. That at least at ever stage, the current stage of consciousness can be broken down into a limited number of thoughts which make for a definite and complete image of consciousness at an instant of time, but which will seem greater than life as it changes between moment and moment.

Both of these ideas can only be born from the idea that consciousness itself is material, resolutely bounded by time and space just like a regular material phenomenon. However, these ideas are born from an inchoent perspective of universal materialism which develops through the ill-fitting application of material paradigms onto something completely unrelated.

Take the example of your own conscious experience: do you experience thoughts in definite chronological order, or have you simply been made to believe this? You might answer first, that you do experience thoughts in chronological order, but not in a definite way, as it is extremely difficult to distinguish between one thought and the other. Then it is not definite, but what about order? You might answer, that naturally you are not limited to experience one thought at once, as you can respond to multiple events at once, and that it is possible for you to think about two phenomena simultaneously.

Then upon further asking, one can find out that time itself is ill-fitting as a paradigm for thoughts. That for every axiom of time, we have to provide exceptions for consciousness. Then, what about the most basic axiom which is the order of cause and effect. That we only respond to things after they have occurred and not before. However, this is also an arbitration, as we can often times experience foreshadowing thoughts, or predictions, which will often respond to events in the future, and these thoughts can be found in strategic thinking.

As for the bounds of space, finiteness on consciousness, we should ask whether thoughts are atoms, or also composite? Can thoughts be divided themselves into stages? We already know that thoughts are not definite, and we know that thoughts themselves can be divided into parts and those parts into parts on and on.

We find out that all the experiences of consciousness are not immediately observable, never immediately observable, uncontained, unbounded by time or space, and can continue to be explored forever and ever.

3. Analysing the infinite

It seems then quite impossible to analyse something which is at every moment infinite, and as well something for which time is not a fitting metaphor. And we begin to realise that at least time and space provided consistent explanations of what happened, provided we impose arbitrary boundaries on our thoughts.

But as it stands, we do experience our reality in time, and simultaneously the way we think is not chronological as we think it was, but rather experienced chronologically. So we can at least understand that we experience in chronology, but what we are experiencing is not chronological in the traditional sense. So we can still use time as a paradigm for conscious experience, but remember that it does not in any way represent consciousness itself.

Rather, it's better to model consciousness as a big sea of thoughts, through which our process of sailing is conscious experience, and as we move, it seems to us that we are experiencing things in the order that they should come.

In the relative view of thoughts, we can provide the paradigm that every thought is an expression of an idea. These thoughts provide for deviations from the context — which consist of 'previous' thoughts — and are born from voids called prompts. And every new thought is inducted into the context. Overall, a group of similar deviations make for a motion that changes the nature of a context — the change being an equivalent expression for the context having changed from subject to subject.

And we should remember these are all paradigms at least to bind and analyse consciousness through conscious experience. In reality, consciousness is a large sea of thoughts, and we are apprehending thoughts as they pass. As for every group of thoughts that make for a motion, and every group of motions similar that make a large tide we are best satisfied to call context.

We analyse how thoughts relate to each other, and moving into the nature of thoughts is that they themselves are expressions of ideas which are not immediately observable, but can be represented through those thoughts. Whilst not all of us think in human languages, we can be sure to know that conscious experience is expressed in a language, which in all of its expressions is hidden a representation of the essence of the idea.

And the experience part of thoughts should also be well defined. In that, we are vessels for thoughts, which can at some moments increase in the capacity to think and hold thoughts, and at other times decrease. Our brain is the lung of consciousness, from which an emergent approach to the essence of ideas happens, through language, the bridge between the thought and the thinker, the real and the unreal, the staircase from the true to the arbitrary, the encoding of the meaningful through the meaningless. That our material brains have neural networks which are able to express language that comes ever closer to a true idea. This itself the fascinating beginning of the joy of awareness.

I hope that through this, I was able to inspire some thought, and substantially suggest that consciousness is emergent, parallel to the material world. With this, we can hopefully analyse our minds better, and evolve our consciousness. To enjoy awareness, for me, is to know that you are your own thoughts, and to discover ever unfolding worlds within ideas. Even the world around us is within our consciousness, so by understanding ourselves, we understand our world.
